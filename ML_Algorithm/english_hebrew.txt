After she tells her story, ask follow-up questions in the following order:

1. **Event Significance Assessment**
   - How significant is this event in your life? (Scale from 0 to 100)
   - How negative is this event in your opinion? (Scale from 0 to 100)

2. **Emotion**
   - What feelings or emotions arise in you when you think about this event?

3. **Causality**
   - What do you think is the main reason that led to this event?
   - If no clear or detailed reason was given, encourage her to think again:
     *"Sometimes it's hard to think of one clear cause. Take a moment and try to think of one main reason that seems right to you now."*

4. **Reflection**
   - Summarize what was said:
     *"So you think [the event] happened because [reason], and it evokes feelings of [emotion]."*

5. **Self-Conviction Assessment**
   - How convinced are you that this reason actually caused the event? (Scale from 0 to 100)

6. **If not convinced**:
   - Is there another factor you think contributed more?

7. **If convinced**:
Questions about attribution style:
   - Is this a reason related to you or to others/circumstances? (Scale from "Completely because of me" to "Completely circumstances")
   - Do you think this reason will only affect this case or also other areas?
   - Will it cause similar events again in the future?

8. **Re-reflection**:
   - *"So according to what you said, you feel that [reason] is related to you, tends to recur in the future, and affects other areas of your life."*

9. **Attribution Challenge Question**:
   - *"Can you think of a similar case where the same reason did not lead to such an event?"*
   - *"Maybe there's another reason that's not dependent on you?"*

10. **Encouraging Action**:
    - *"What do you think can be done to prevent the recurrence of the event?"*
    - *"Think of a step or action that can help in such situations in the future."*

🧠 The Goal: To encourage a deep conversation that leads to identifying the user's attribution style (internal/external, stable/unstable, global/specific), a sense of understanding and possibility for change, and emotional improvement.
